# P0 make kit: one-session runbook (woven button-up crop)

**Status: DRAFT.** The steps come from the draft recipe `woven_shirt_crop_v1` (PR #11, `content/recipes/woven_shirt_crop_v1/recipe.yaml`, status `draft`, `reviewed_by: null`). Every tolerance and hem fold below is a draft value. **Until a named sewist signs the recipe off (task H-02), this session is a trial of the draft, not a validation of the recipe.** Write the sewist's name on the record if one reviews it before or after.

Why this exists: the P0 exit gate in the blueprint (§12) needs at least one actual user-measured woven shirt with an internally consistent, reviewed, dimensioned cut plan that is physically trialled, and the deviation recorded. Gate G1 (§18.3) needs a human ruler check. This sheet is the one-session procedure; fill in `physical-make-record.md` (or the CSV) as you go.

Print this page. Plan about 2 to 3 hours for a first make. Two people help: one measures, one writes down.

## 1. What to bring

Required (from the draft recipe):

- [ ] Woven, non-stretch button-up shirt (poplin, linen blend). Washed and dried first, so it shrinks before you measure.
- [ ] Straight ruler or metal rule, 50 cm or longer, with mm marks. Note its make: ______
- [ ] Tailor's chalk or chalk pencil
- [ ] Sharp fabric scissors
- [ ] Pins or clips
- [ ] Iron and pressing surface
- [ ] Sewing machine with straight stitch, **or** a hand needle (slip stitch). Matching thread.

Also bring (for the record):

- [ ] Phone for photos, a pen, this sheet, a flat clean table at least 90 cm long
- [ ] A second ruler or tape for the independent check (step 6)
- [ ] A stopwatch or phone timer
- [ ] Spare woven fabric (optional) for practice stitching

**Check eligibility before you start.** Stop and pick another shirt if: it is stretch or knit; a pocket, pleat, vent or seam would cross the cut line; a button or buttonhole would fall between the cut line and the finished edge; or the bottom edge is strongly curved or shirt-tail. Record the reason if you reject a shirt.

## 2. Photos to take (before cutting)

All on the same table, shirt buttoned and smoothed flat, ruler visible in frame. Note file names, not people.

- [ ] P1 Front, whole shirt, straight down from above
- [ ] P2 Back, whole shirt, same framing
- [ ] P3 Close-up of the shoulder landmark with the ruler zero on it
- [ ] P4 Close-up of the label (composition, size). Cover any name.
- [ ] P5 Bottom edge, showing whether it is straight
- [ ] P6 Any flaw, stain, repair, pocket or seam near the cut zone

## 3. Measurements: take each one twice

**One landmark for everything.** The **shoulder landmark** is the top of the shoulder where it meets the collar (the highest point next to the neck), on the left side as you look at the front. Use the same side every time. Put the ruler's zero end exactly on it. Look straight down at the ruler. Read to the nearest mm. Lift the ruler away between repeats. If two readings differ, take a third and note why.

Write millimetres. 1 cm = 10 mm.

| # | Measurement | Named landmark to named landmark | Reading 1 | Reading 2 | Reading 3 (if they differ) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | Original length, front | Shoulder landmark down the front, beside the button strip, to the bottom edge at centre front | | | |
| M2 | Original length, back | Same shoulder landmark (turned over), straight down the back centre to the bottom edge | | | |
| M3 | Chest flat width (context only) | Armpit seam to armpit seam, shirt flat | | | |
| M4 | Bottom edge flat width (context only) | Left side seam to right side seam at the hem | | | |
| M5 | Lowest buttonhole position | Shoulder landmark to the centre of the lowest buttonhole | | | |

M5 matters: the cut line minus the hem allowance must sit **below** the lowest button/buttonhole (eligibility rule). Also record the bottom edge's evenness: lay the ruler along it, largest gap ____ mm.

**Spread** (reading 1 minus reading 2, absolute): M1 ____ mm. M2 ____ mm. This spread is your measurement noise, and it sets how much of the final error is just measuring. Record it. Do not hide a large one.

## 4. Decide the change and work out the planned cut mark

1. Decide the **finished length** you want, in mm, from the shoulder landmark to the finished bottom edge (call it **F**). Write it before you do any cutting: F = ______ mm.
2. Hem allowance **H**. The draft recipe default is 30 mm (a 10 mm first fold plus a 20 mm second fold). H = ______ mm. If you change it, say why.
3. **Planned cut mark = F + H** (millimetres from the shoulder landmark, per the draft recipe formula `finished_length_mm + hem_allowance_mm`).

> Worked example. **Illustrative only, not data:** F = 500 mm, H = 30 mm gives a cut mark of 530 mm. If the original length is 780 mm, 780 minus 530 = 250 mm of fabric is removed, which is positive, so the cut is possible.

F = ______ H = ______ **Cut mark = ______ mm**
Original length (average of M1 readings) = ______ mm. Fabric to remove = original minus cut mark = ______ mm. Must be positive and the line must clear M5.

If the app or the feasibility engine produces a cut plan, write its design revision ID here: ______ and copy its cut mark: ______ mm. If the app's number and your hand sum disagree, stop and find out why; record it.

## 5. Mark the shirt

1. Shirt buttoned and flat. Measure down from the shoulder landmark by the cut mark and put a small chalk dot. Repeat near the left side seam, near the right side seam, and once near each edge of the button strip.
2. Lay the ruler along the dots, draw one straight chalk line across the front. If a dot is off the line, rub it out and remeasure that spot.
3. Turn the shirt over (still buttoned), measure from the same landmark on the back, dot and join the same way.

- [ ] I held the shirt up. This is the length I want, and the finished shirt will be shorter than the chalk line by H.
- [ ] Time ruler check completed: ____

**If any check fails: rub out the chalk and mark again. Do not cut until all pass.** If a check fails and you re-mark, note each attempt: the number of re-marks is a result. Cutting goes ahead only when a human has ticked this box (gate G1): Checked by: ______

## 7. Cut, press, hem

Follow the draft recipe steps in order: cut along the line (front, then back); then check the cut edge (**step 7a**); press the hem in two folds (10 mm then 20 mm, draft values); sew by machine or hand; press.

**7a. After cutting, before any trimming.** Measure landmark to cut edge in three places front and three back. Largest distance from the chalk line (draft limit 3 mm; beyond it, stop and use the tailor route): ____ mm. Write the six readings on the record.

Note times for: marking ____ min, cutting ____ min, pressing ____ min, sewing ____ min. Note any rework (unpicking, re-pressing, re-cutting), what caused it, and anything you needed help with or found confusing in the instructions.

## 8. What to measure afterwards

Shirt buttoned, flat, smoothed. Same landmark, same side, same ruler, same method as step 3. Each twice.

| # | Measurement | Reading 1 | Reading 2 |

| A2 | Finished length, back | | |
| A3 | Cut-edge position before hemming: landmark to cut edge (compare with the cut mark) | | |
| A4 | Hem depth as sewn, three places | | |
| A5 | Bottom edge flat width (compare with M4; should be unchanged if cut straight) | | |

Also check and record yes/no: hem even, stitching secure, bottom button still closes, any raw edge showing. Photograph the finished shirt flat with the ruler in frame (P7 front, P8 back, P9 hem close-up).

## 9. After the session

- [ ] Fill in `physical-make-record.md` (or add rows to `physical-make-record.template.csv`). Copy numbers as written. **Do not round, tidy or drop a reading, and keep bad results.**
- [ ] For each dimension: predicted (what the plan said), actual (what you measured), then absolute error = |actual − predicted|, in mm. Give a cause in words, and say whether the system's checks would have prevented it. If unsure, write "unknown".
- [ ] Record maker confidence (1 to 5), perceived difficulty (1 to 5), time, help needed, which instructions need changing.
- [ ] Record consent and which photos may be used in the report or demo and the deletion terms.
- [ ] Run `python tests/benchmarks/analysis/make_report.py <your csv> --out <dir>` to get the table and chart. One make is one make: the report states its sample size and does not claim a pattern.

## needs-human

Nothing in this kit can be done by an agent. In order:

1. **Sewist sign-off of the recipe draft** (H-02): a named sewist reviews `woven_shirt_crop_v1`, confirms or changes the fold depths and the 2 mm / 3 mm tolerances, and a human changes its status. Without this the trial is of a draft.
2. **Choose and bring the shirt**, wash it, and do all measuring, marking, cutting and sewing.
3. **The G1 ruler check** (step 6) ticked by a person.
4. **Consent**: whoever is photographed or helps, and the garment owner.
5. **Agree the tolerance** with the sewist (blueprint §6.3/§17.4: agree thresholds before experiments). This kit defines none. Agree it **before** looking at the results.
6. **Fill in the record** and upload photos to the private store.
7. Decide whether this single make is enough for the 10 Nov interim report (it supports "one make was trialled", nothing more) or whether more makes are needed (blueprint §12, P3, proposes a target of four physical makes in total).
