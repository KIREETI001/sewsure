# Tailor interview guide (25–30 minutes)

For: founders. Participant code: `T__`. Date: ____ Founder: ____ Note-taker: ____

**Goal:** learn how alteration requests reach tailors now, what information is missing, how they quote, what a useful brief looks like, and whether a pilot and a short letter of intent are realistic. We are learning, not selling. Do not promise customers, volume or income.

**Rules for the interviewer**
- Ask about the last few real jobs, not general opinions.
- Do not name prices of our own. Do not suggest what a good brief contains until they have described it.
- Do not record names of their customers. If they mention one, leave it out of notes.
- Confirm consent (see `consent-form-draft.md`; business-use wording applies) and audio preference before starting.

Mark each note slot with their words where possible.

## 0. Opening (2 min)

Ask: "Are you 18 or over?" (if no, thank and end). Read: "We are students building an app that helps people redesign clothes they own, with a tailor option for jobs people don't want to do themselves. We want to understand how your work actually runs. There are no right answers. You may skip anything. May I take notes? Optional audio?"

- Consent in tracker: ☐   Audio: ☐ yes ☐ no

## 1. About the work (3 min)

1.1 Tell me about your work: what kinds of jobs do you do most?
> Notes:

1.2 How many alteration or redesign requests do you handle in a typical week? What is a busy week?
> Notes (number, period):

1.3 Which jobs do you enjoy or avoid? Why?
> Notes:

## 2. How requests arrive (5 min)

2.1 Tell me about the last three requests you received. How did each one reach you?
> Notes (walk-in / phone / message / referral / other):

2.2 What does the customer usually bring or show you?
> Notes:

2.3 What happens between first contact and starting the work?
> Notes:

2.4 How often do customers ask for something that can't be done? What happens then?
> Notes:

## 3. What's missing (5 min)

3.1 What information do you usually have to ask for that the customer didn't give?
> Notes:

3.2 Tell me about a job that went wrong or took longer because of missing or wrong information.
> Notes:

3.3 Do you ever need photos or measurements before quoting? What makes them useful or useless?
> Notes:

3.4 What can you tell from a photo, and what must you see in person?
> Notes:

## 4. Quoting and payment (4 min)

4.1 How do you decide a price? Walk me through the last quote.
> Notes:

4.2 Do you give quotes before seeing the garment? When, and how accurate are they?
> Notes:

4.3 How do deposits, timings and collection work?
> Notes:

4.4 What do customers get wrong about price or time?
> Notes:

## 5. What a useful brief contains (4 min)

5.1 If a customer sent you a request before coming in, what would you want in it? Ask them to list; then ask what is most important.
> Notes:

5.2 What would make you ignore a request?
> Notes:

5.3 Of the things you listed, which do you need first, and in what form? (Do not offer items they did not mention; probe only after they finish.)
> Notes:

5.4 After you've seen the brief: what would help you give a quote or say no quickly?
> Notes:

## 6. Interest in a pilot (4 min)

6.1 How do customers currently find you? What works best?
> Notes:

6.2 Tell me about any new way of getting customers or requests that you have tried. How did it go?
> Notes:

6.3 How do you decide whether to take part in something new, like a trial? What do you need to know first?
> Notes:

6.4 Tell me about something you stopped using or doing in your business. Why?
> Notes:

6.5 Is there anything you would want agreed in writing before trying something new with customers?
> Notes:

## 7. Letter of intent (2 min)

Read: "We may ask interested tailors to sign a short non-binding letter saying they are willing to take part in a trial, if it goes ahead. It does not commit you to pay or to accept work."

7.1 How do you feel about that? What would you need to decide? Who else, if anyone, would be involved?
> Notes (yes / maybe / no; reason):

7.2 What would you want it to say or not say?
> Notes:

Do not request a signature in the session. Log interest in the tracker; a founder follows up with the draft later.

## 8. Close (1 min)

8.1 Anything we should have asked?
> Notes:

Read: "Thank you. Notes will be de-identified. You can ask us to delete your data any time."
